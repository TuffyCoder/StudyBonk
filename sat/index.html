Questions students also ask

How is the digital SAT structured?
Two sections — Reading & Writing and Math — each split into two adaptive modules. Reading & Writing is 54 questions in 64 minutes; Math is 44 questions in 70 minutes. Your performance on the first module of each section sets the difficulty of the second.
Is the digital SAT easier than the paper SAT?
Shorter and less reading-heavy, yes — but 'easier for everyone' doesn't mean a better score for you, since scores are curved against other testers. The adaptive format rewards consistency: careless mistakes on easy first-module questions can route you to an easier second module with a lower score ceiling.
What should I study first for the SAT?
Take one timed practice test in Bluebook first to find your baseline, then attack your weakest section. Grammar rules and math fundamentals give the fastest score gains because they're learnable systems, not talents. Vocabulary and reading strategy come next.
Can you use a calculator on the SAT?
Yes, on the entire Math section. The Bluebook app includes a built-in Desmos graphing calculator, and you may also bring your own approved calculator. Calculator strategy is now a real test skill — graphing a system is often faster than solving it by hand.
How many times should I take the SAT?
Most students take it 2–3 times across junior and senior year. Scores often rise between attempts simply from exposure to real testing conditions, and most colleges superscore — combining your best section scores across dates.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Is the SAT still a thing now that it's digital?
Yes — the SAT went fully digital in spring 2024 in the US. It's shorter (about 2 hours 14 minutes), adaptive (the second module adjusts to your first-module performance), and taken on a laptop or tablet in the Bluebook app. Hundreds of US colleges still accept scores, and many use them for scholarships and course placement.
What is a good digital SAT score?
Scores still run 400–1600. Around 1050 is the national average, 1200 opens doors at many state schools, and 1350+ is competitive for highly selective colleges. The smart move is to look up the middle-50% range at the colleges you like and set your target 50–100 points above where a free practice test says you are now.
How long should I study for the SAT?
Most students need 20–80 focused hours depending on their starting point and goal. That's roughly a 1-week sprint for triage, a 1-month plan for a solid bump, or a 3-month build for a big jump. Daily 20–30 minute sessions with flashcards and quizzes beat weekend cramming — spaced practice is how scores stick.
Can I prepare for the SAT for free?
Completely. StudyBonk's guides, decks and quizzes are free with no account, the Bluebook app offers official full-length adaptive practice tests, and Khan Academy has official question sets. A free plan plus honest error review beats an expensive course you never finish.
Does StudyBonk store my SAT progress anywhere?
Only on your device. StudyBonk has no accounts, no tracking and no ads — your streaks, XP and flashcard progress live in your browser's local storage, and the app works offline after the first load. Your prep is nobody's business but yours.
